# Programming the 8051 parts

Four chips, three different routes.

| Part | Flash | Route | Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AT89S52** | 8 KB | SPI ISP | USB-ISP HID + `python -m niusburner` |
| **STC89C52RC** | 8 KB | serial bootloader | `stcgal` (same DIP-40 header does not ACK SPI) |
| **STC15W408AS** | 8 KB | serial bootloader **only** | `stcgal` + USB-TTL |
| **AT89C2051** | 2 KB | 12 V parallel | [Nano programmer](#at89c2051--nano-v3-programmer) |

> **Read [the footprint section](#footprint-what-fits-and-what-does-not)
> before planning a project.** Segment and character displays fit the 8 KB
> parts comfortably (4,677 B). The graphics path does not fit any of them yet.

---

## Parts

`python -m niusburner boards` lists them; `boards --features` says which
peripherals each has.

| part | flash | route | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| AT89S52 | 8 KB | USB-ISP, serial ISP | `verified` here |
| AT89S51 | 4 KB | the same | `implemented` |
| AT89S53 | 12 KB | the same | `implemented` |
| AT89S8252 | 8 KB + 2 KB EEPROM | the same | `implemented` |
| AT89S8253 | 12 KB + 2 KB EEPROM | the same | `implemented` |
| AT89C2051 | 2 KB | 12 V parallel, on a Nano | compile only |
| STC89C52RC | 8 KB | UART bootloader (`stcgal`) | `verified` here |
| STC89C51RC | 4 KB | the same | `implemented` |
| STC90C52RC | 8 KB | the same | `implemented` |

`implemented` means the route is wired and the protocol is the one the part
documents, but no part of that type has been in the socket on this bench.
The AT89S52 and the STC89C52RC lines are `verified`: both have been
programmed and run here.

The AT89S family all speak the same serial ISP: `AC 53` to enable, `20` and
`40` to read and write a byte. The sizes and signatures differ and nothing
else does, which is why they share a backend. The EEPROM on the 8252/8253 is
a separate address space and is not written by this tool.

### STC parts are a different protocol

An STC89C52RC drops into the same DIP-40 socket and runs the same
instruction set, and none of that helps: it has no SPI programming interface
at all. The ISP header cannot reach it. It is written through its own UART
bootloader, which means the serial adapter, not the IDC10 cable:

```bash
python -m niusburner upload <sketch> --board stc89c52rc --yes --port COM31
```

That bootloader listens only in the moments right after reset, and this
generation has no way in from software: no pin to assert, nothing to send.
The supply is the only door, so the board has to be **power-cycled while
the upload is already running**, and the surest order is to remove power
first, start the upload, then restore it -- the window opens once and is
about a second wide. `stcgal` does the protocol; install it with
`python -m pip install stcgal`.

Two consequences that cost an afternoon here, both worth knowing before
wiring a bench:

**Two supplies cannot power-cycle each other.** With a programmer and a
serial adapter both feeding VDD, interrupting either leaves the rail up and
no power-on happens. This is also why an AT89S52 is untroubled by any of
it: that part is programmed through the ISP header's RST pin, which the
programmer owns outright whoever supplies power.

**The ISP header cannot do it either.** Its `0x0D` frame carries a VCC
byte, and setting it low was measured against a part printing once a
second: the printing continued straight through a 1.5 s "cut". That byte
does not gate the rail.

### What an automatic upload needs

VDD has to be switchable by software. That is a hardware property, and
three things on this bench turned out not to have it:

- **The board's own power switch** is mechanical, and on this board it does
  not even carry the programmer's rail: the dongle feeds VDD directly.
- **The programmer's VCC pin** looked promising, because its `0x0D` frame
  carries a byte that reads like a VCC flag. It is not one. Held low for
  eight seconds, with the serial adapter's TXD held low as well so it could
  not back-feed through the RXD clamp diode, a part printing once a second
  kept printing. The pin is tied to USB 5 V.
- **Unplugging the adapter** does cycle the board, and closes the port the
  bootloader has to talk on.

Worth knowing while diagnosing this: every 5 V source touching any pin
keeps the part alive, because an input pin clamps to VCC through an ESD
diode. EA tied to a separate 5 V rail did it here, and so did the serial
adapter's idle-high TXD. Chasing the supply is pointless until every one of
those is on the same rail or held low.

So: for an upload nobody has to touch, VDD needs a switch under electrical
control -- a P-channel MOSFET or PNP in the supply driven by the adapter's
DTR or RTS, which is what every commercial STC board already has. Then:

```bash
python -m niusburner upload <sketch> --board stc89c52rc --yes     --port COM31 --reset-pin dtr
```

and in the IDE, **Tools > Power switch > Adapter DTR switches VDD**.

Because the bootloader starts the new firmware itself, an STC upload cannot
hold the part in reset the way the ISP route does — so the first line a
sketch prints can be gone before a monitor attaches.

## The USB-ISP programmer

The IDC10 header is the standard AVR ISP pinout, printed on the case:

```
MOSI  1  2  VCC
NC    3  4  GND
RST   5  6  GND
SCK   7  8  GND
MISO  9  10 GND
```

### AT89S52 wiring

| IDC10 | Signal | DIP40 pin | Chip signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MOSI | 6 | P1.5 |
| 9 | MISO | 7 | P1.6 |
| 7 | SCK | 8 | P1.7 |
| 5 | RST | 9 | RST |
| 2 | VCC | 40 | VCC |
| 4, 6, 8, 10 | GND | 20 | GND |

**A crystal is mandatory on the AT89S52.** 11.0592 MHz across XTAL1/XTAL2
(pins 19/18) with two 22 pF capacitors to ground. Without a running clock the
part does not answer the programmer, and the failure is indistinguishable from
bad wiring.

**RST needs a 1 kΩ pulldown to GND** (DIP-40 pin 9, same net as IDC10 pin 5).
AT89S52 reset is active-high. This USB-ISP is an AVR core: after programming
it only Hi-Zs RST, and its pull-up holds the 8051 in reset. HID has no
push-pull-low encoding. The pulldown loses to the dongle during ISP (RST
driven high) and wins after `release_to_run` (Hi-Z). Unplugging the IDC10
is not the fix.

### Software

Headless CLI only. The happy path is one command from a sketch directory:

```bash
python -m niusburner setup --board at89s52
python -m niusburner upload examples/at89s52_blink --board at89s52 --yes
```

`--yes` is the erase acknowledgement. `compile` (no flash) and the low-level
`probe` / `flash` / `build-mcs51` commands remain for when you already have
an image. See [docs/workflow.md](../workflow.md).

Expected signature: `1E 52 06`. `avrdude` does not speak this 8051 ISP
framing. The dongle (VID `03EB` / PID `C8B4`) stays on **HidUsb**; do not
install WinUSB via Zadig.

### STC89C52RC on the same header

STC89C52RC silicon does not ACK `AC 53 00 00`.  Use the serial bootloader:

```bash
pip install stcgal
stcgal -P stc89 -p <PORT> firmware.hex
```

## STC15W408AS — serial only

This part has **no SPI ISP**. The USB-ISP cannot reach it.

| USB-TTL | STC15W408AS |
|---|---|
| TXD | P3.0 / RXD |
| RXD | P3.1 / TXD |
| GND | GND |
| 5 V or 3.3 V | VCC |

```bash
stcgal -P stc15 -p <PORT> firmware.hex
```

`stcgal` prints `waiting for MCU` and then **you must power-cycle the board** —
the bootloader only listens in the first few milliseconds after reset. Pulling
VCC and reconnecting it is the whole procedure.

No crystal needed; the part has an internal oscillator.

## AT89C2051 — Nano V3 programmer

This chip has **no ISP of any kind**. It is not in the USB-ISP's supported
list, and that is not an omission: the silicon has no serial programming
interface. It is programmed by a 12 V parallel algorithm.

Everything needed to *build* the programmer is in
[`hardware/nano_at89c2051/`](../../hardware/nano_at89c2051/).

### What makes it awkward

**There is no address bus.** The internal address counter resets when RST goes
low and advances one step per pulse on XTAL1. Programming is strictly
sequential from address 0 — you cannot seek, and rewriting a single byte means
walking the counter back to it from zero.

### Wiring

| Nano | DIP20 pin | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| D2–D9 | 12–19 | P1.0–P1.7 (data bus) |
| D10 | 5 | XTAL1 — counter advance |
| D11 | 6 | P3.2 `/PROG` |
| D12 | 7 | P3.3 ┐ |
| D13 | 8 | P3.4 │ mode select |
| A0 | 9 | P3.5 │ |
| A1 | 11 | P3.7 ┘ |
| A2 | — | enable for the **12 V** switch onto RST (pin 1) |
| A3 | — | enable for the **5 V** path onto RST (pin 1) |
| 5V | 20 | VCC |
| GND | 10 | GND |

### The 12 V rail

**The Nano cannot generate 12 V.** RST/VPP needs it during write and erase.
Use a boost module or a bench supply, switched by a transistor driven from A2.
A3 drives a separate plain-5 V path for the modes that want V<sub>IH</sub>.

A workable switch is a PNP or P-channel device on the 12 V rail with an NPN
level-shifter from A2, and a diode-OR or second transistor for the 5 V path.
Whatever the arrangement:

> ### ⚠️ A2 and A3 must never be high together
>
> That connects 12 V to the Nano's 5 V rail and destroys it — and probably the
> USB port feeding it.
>
> `assertRst()` in the firmware drives **both** low and waits before raising
> either, so software cannot cause it. **Build the hardware so it cannot
> happen either** — if one transistor can be on while the other is, a stuck
> pin at power-up is enough.

### Procedure

```bash
# 1. Flash the programmer firmware onto the Nano (once). This is the Nano,
#    not the AT89C2051 — arduino-cli, not NiusBurner.
arduino-cli compile -b arduino:avr:nano --upload -p <PORT> \
    hardware/nano_at89c2051/nano_at89c2051.ino

# 2. Compile the target sketch (upload of this board is not wired yet)
python -m niusburner compile <sketch> --board at89c2051

# 3. When the 12 V backend exists:
python -m niusburner probe at89c2051 --confirm at89c2051
python -m niusburner flash at89c2051 .niusburner/at89c2051/firmware.ihx \
  --confirm at89c2051 --ack-data-loss --state-policy replace
```

The signature step costs a second and separates "the 12 V rail is not
switching" from "my code is wrong". On these parts that is most of the
information you get back, so do not skip it.

Every byte is verified as it is written, and a failure reports the exact
address rather than surfacing later as a corrupt image.

## Serial console

The AT89S52 hardware UART is on P3.0/P3.1. A CH341 USB-TTL is a **second**
cable next to the USB-ISP header:

| CH341 (5 V) | AT89S52 |
|---|---|
| TXD | P3.0 / RXD (pin 10) |
| RXD | P3.1 / TXD (pin 11) |
| GND | GND |

```bash
python -m niusburner upload examples/at89s52_serial --board at89s52 --yes --port COM31
```

`upload --port` programs, holds the part in reset, opens the port and only
then releases it, so the sketch's first line is not lost while the port is
still opening. `python -m niusburner monitor --port COM31 --baud 9600`
attaches to an already-running board. The USB-ISP programmer is HID; the
CH341 is the only COM port.

Before blaming the toolchain when a verified image prints nothing, check
EA/VPP (pin 31) is tied to VCC — see
[../wiring/usbasp-idc10.md](../wiring/usbasp-idc10.md).

The AT89S52 and the STC parts have room for real logging. **The AT89C2051
does not** — with 2 KB of flash a handful of `printf` calls is a measurable
fraction of the budget. Treat it as a deployment target, not a development
one: get the code right on a bigger part first.

## Footprint: what fits and what does not

### ✅ Segment and character displays fit

A TM1637 needs no graphics stack — `nd_tm1637.h` includes only `nd_hal.h`.

| Flags | CODE |
|---|---|
| `-DND_TINY=1` | 4,677 B |
| `+ -DND_HAL_BUSES=0` | **3,419 B** |

3,419 B is **42% of an 8 KB part**, leaving most of the flash for your
application. Build a segment project with:

```
-DND_TINY=1 -DND_HAL_BUSES=0
```

`ND_HAL_BUSES=0` removes SPI, I2C and parallel from the port HAL — nine of its
sixteen functions, which a two-wire TM1637 never touches. SDCC links them
anyway because it eliminates dead code per module, not per function.

Prefer `nd_tm1637_show_i16()` over `show_int()`: a 4-digit module cannot exceed
9999, and the 32-bit form costs about 1,600 bytes of SDCC runtime.

### The AT89C2051 (2 KB) still does not fit

3,419 B is 1.7x over. The remaining bulk is the TM1637 driver's own nine public
functions — init, brightness, display on/off, raw write, clear, integer, time,
and the segment table. A hand-written routine that does exactly one thing is
around 300 bytes, and that is the honest comparison: the gap is API surface,
not inefficiency.

Two notes for anyone trying to close it:

- `--model-small` does **not** help. It fails to link — *"could not get 54
  consecutive bytes in internal RAM"* — because the part has 128 bytes of IRAM
  and no XRAM at all. `--model-large` with `--stack-auto` is what links.
- Dropping to `init` + `show_i16` + `brightness` alone would get close to 2 KB,
  but at that point a purpose-written routine is the better engineering.

### ⚠️ The graphics path does not fit yet

Measured with SDCC, not estimated:

| Build | CODE | AT89C2051 (2 KB) | The 8 KB parts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full | 38,003 B | ✗ | ✗ |
| `-DND_TINY=1` | 24,123 B | ✗ | ✗ |

`ND_TINY` drops shapes, text and bitmaps — a 36% saving that is still three
times too large. The cause is `nd_u32` buffer arithmetic pulling in
`__divslong`, `__divulong`, `__modulong` and `__mullong`; 32-bit division on
an 8-bit CPU is a called routine every time.

Fitting 8 KB needs a DIRECT-only build, 16-bit counts on 8-bit targets, and
compiling only the bus transport in use. Estimated 6–9 KB. **Not done yet** —
tracked as the top priority.
